A day in it

Six screens, one working day

Punch in, apply for leave, raise an advance, approve someone else's, grade the month, run payroll. Each one below is a genuine capture — click it for the numbers behind it.

01 · Attendance

A month you can read at a glance

Punches come from the Android app with a photo and a GPS fix, because that is the only place a punch can be trusted. Everything else — the monthly calendar, the daily punch list, miss-punch regularisation — lives on the web where HR actually works.

Geo-tagged punches with the captured address stored against each one
Week offs and leave shaded separately from genuine absence
Miss-punch raised by the employee and cleared by the manager, in the same place
02 · Leave

Nine leave types, balances that are never a guess

Every type your policy actually has — earned, casual, sick, monthly holiday, restricted holiday, maternity, menstrual, work-from-home, leave without pay — each with its own quota, its own approval route and a balance the employee can see before they apply.

Comp-off earned against a worked holiday and consumed like any other type
Team approvals on the same page a manager applies from
Leave-without-pay flows straight into the payroll run as a deduction
03 · Approvals

One queue. Everything waiting on you.

Expense notes, advances, settlements, leave, resignations and performance ratings all run through the same approval engine, so a manager has one list rather than six. Each card says why it is with you — and Query / Hold sends it back with a reason instead of killing it.

Six stages — reporting manager, finance initiator, project leader, business leader, finance, final
Stages that do not apply are bypassed, not left hanging
A stage can only be actioned once — two approvers clicking together is a safe race
04 · Payroll

Payroll that has read the attendance register

Generate the whole company in one run. Mid-month joiners and approved exits are prorated by date, approved leave-without-pay is deducted, and days with neither a punch nor approved leave are flagged for a human rather than silently docked.

Published payslips lock — a re-run skips them rather than overwriting
Per-employee components override the company template where they need to
Bank sheet export and a PDF payslip in the portal and the app
05 · Performance

A grade the person can see coming

Monthly KPIs with weighted KRAs, a self-rating, then a functional and a business review. The PLI percentage and the letter grade fall out of the numbers rather than out of a conversation, and last month locks once it is signed off.

Grades from OAT to SBT, derived from the weighted score
Twelve months on one strip on the employee's own dashboard
The same approval engine, so a rating cannot skip its reviewer
06 · Structure

One ladder for the whole group

Departments, designations and levels belong to the organisation, not to each legal entity inside it — so "HR Manager" means the same thing however the person is paid. Grade is the code on the title; level is where that title sits. Both are kept, because they are not the same question.

Build the ladder from existing grades in one click, then rename the rungs
Removing a rung unhooks its titles rather than blocking the restructure
People inherit their level from their title — nothing to set per person

Platform

Built for groups, not for one office

Several legal entities, one set of people, and a wall between your data and everybody else's that is enforced in the query, not in the user interface.

One tenant per organisation

Every read filters on the organisation. A company id from another tenant is refused even when the person guessing it is an admin of their own.

Several companies inside it

Payroll, statutory registers and cost centres are per legal entity. Structure, policy and people are shared across the group.

Roles you define

Module-level permissions on custom roles, on top of the system ones. Creating logins and adding companies stay with the owner.

Subscription modules

Thirty-six modules, enabled per organisation. A module that is off is off in the API as well as in the menu.

A direct line to us

Any login, in any tenant, can raise a master ticket with a screenshot. It reaches us and nobody else — not even their own admins.

Web and Android, one account

The app handles what needs a phone — punches with a photo and a GPS fix, payslips, approvals on the move. Everything else is the same account on the web.

Security

Boring, on purpose

The parts of an HR system that must not be interesting: what is stored, who can read it, and what happens when somebody tries something.

PII encrypted at restPAN, Aadhaar and bank details are AES-encrypted in the database and are deliberately not decrypted into any bulk export.
Reads are recordedOpening a sensitive number on somebody's own screen writes an audit entry against the person who looked.
Uploads are checked, not trustedType comes from an allowlist with a magic-byte check, and every attachment is served as a download so it can never execute on our own domain.
Rate-limited behind a real proxyLimits key off the genuine client address, so a forged forwarding header does not buy anybody extra attempts.
